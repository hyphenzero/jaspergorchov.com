Sometimes the best way to learn a tool is to give yourself a constraint and see what comes out. For this piece, I set myself a simple rule: use only primitive shapes — spheres, cubes, toruses, and cones — and arrange them into a composition that felt intentional, balanced, and evocative.

## The process

I started with a mood board of abstract sculptures and mid-century mobiles, looking for inspiration in how physical objects occupy space. The challenge was translating that sensibility into a digital scene where gravity is optional and materials can be anything.

The final composition centers on a suspended cluster of forms at slightly different scales, each one rotated to catch the light from a three-point setup. The color palette is restrained — soft blues, warm ambers, and neutral greys — letting the shapes and their shadows do most of the work.

## Materials and lighting

The surfaces are a mix of matte and glossy materials, with a few translucent elements to add visual interest. The key light is a large area light positioned to create long, soft shadows that anchor the forms in space. A warm rim light separates the cluster from the background, and a subtle fill light opens up the shadows just enough to reveal the geometry.

I used Freestyle for the line art overlay on the alternate renders, which gives the composition a slightly illustrative quality that contrasts nicely with the photorealistic main render.

## Why abstract?

Abstract 3D work is a sandbox for me — a place to experiment without the pressure of realism or client expectations. Every piece teaches me something about composition, lighting, or material behavior that I carry into more structured projects.
