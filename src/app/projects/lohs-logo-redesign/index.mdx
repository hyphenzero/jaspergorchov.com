Every high school has a logo. Most of them are dating themselves — clip-art mascots, gradient-heavy wordmarks, or the kind of shield that looks like it was rendered in Microsoft Word Art circa 2003. Lake Oswego High School was no exception. I set out to give my school a visual identity that felt as modern and ambitious as the students walking its halls.

## The brief

The existing logo wasn't bad, exactly — it was just tired. The Lakers branding centered on a wooden paddle and anchor motif that had been through too many redesigns by too many committees, and the typography had that slightly squished look that comes from squeezing a long school name into a short horizontal space.

The goal was a refresh, not a revolution. The new mark needed to keep the core Lakers identity intact while bringing the whole system into something cleaner, more consistent, and more versatile — something that would look as good on a letterhead as it would on a hoodie.

## What I designed

The primary mark is a simplified anchor with a paddle integrated into the negative space — a nod to the original motif, but rendered with much cleaner geometry. The anchor is bold enough to stand alone as an avatar or app icon, but also works as part of a horizontal lockup with the school name set in a modern sans-serif.

The color palette was the biggest change. The previous scheme used a dark navy and a bright red that often clashed in practice. I shifted to a deeper, warmer navy with a more versatile accent palette — a muted gold for secondary elements, a slate grey for text, and a vibrant teal for digital applications. The result is a system that feels cohesive across print, web, and apparel.

## Deliverables

Alongside the primary and secondary logo marks, I delivered a full brand guide covering typography, color usage, spacing rules, and do's and don'ts for the mark. I also created simplified versions for mono printing, embroidery, and social media avatars — because a logo that only works in full color on a website isn't really a logo system.
