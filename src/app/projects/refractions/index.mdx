I've always been fascinated by the way light bends as it passes through glass — the way it splits, warps, and recombines into something unexpected. *Refractions* was born from that curiosity, a weekend spent in Blender pushing glass shaders and geometry nodes to see what kinds of forms I could conjure.

## The concept

The scene is built around a cluster of abstract geometric forms, each one faceted and transparent in a slightly different way. Light enters from multiple angles — some sources are visible in the frame, others implied — and the result is a tangle of caustic highlights, colored shadows, and internal reflections that feel almost organic.

I wanted the final render to sit somewhere between architectural and organic — like a model of a crystalline structure you might find in a natural history museum, if nature had access to Cycles and a GPU.

## Technical approach

The glass materials use a mix of principled BSDF shaders with controlled roughness and transmission values, layered over a subtle emissive base to give the forms an inner glow. The environment lighting is a combination of an HDRI for global illumination and a few carefully placed area lights to create the distinct refraction lines you see cutting across the forms.

Rendering was done in Cycles at 4K with caustics enabled. Each frame took around 45 minutes to converge, but the final result was worth the wait.

## What I learned

This project taught me more about light transport than any tutorial ever could. Small tweaks to the index of refraction or the roughness of a surface completely change the character of the piece. It's the kind of hands-on learning that sticks with you.
