For this project, I created a series of three procedurally generated animations in Blender using Geometry Nodes — a tool that lets you modify and duplicate geometry through a visual node-based system. Each animation starts with a simple base shape, instanced across a point cloud and brought to life through noise-driven movement.

<Video src="https://nsitize5xjipsbbp.public.blob.vercel-storage.com/geonodes-1.mp4" />

The first animation features a ring of long cylinders rotating in sync, their overlapping forms creating constantly shifting moiré patterns. The second uses a grid of hexagons whose heights are driven by a noise texture, producing a wave-like surface that ripples and flows.

<Video src="https://nsitize5xjipsbbp.public.blob.vercel-storage.com/geonodes-2.mp4" />

The third starts with a flat pill-shaped profile, extruded into 3D and arranged in a grid, with each instance rotating in a direction determined by the same noise texture — creating a twisting, organic field of forms.

<Video src="https://nsitize5xjipsbbp.public.blob.vercel-storage.com/geonodes-3.mp4" />

## Process

To keep render times manageable, I used Eevee, Blender's realtime physically-based render engine. It produces final frames in a fraction of the time Cycles would take, and while it doesn't match Cycles' photorealistic quality, it's more than capable for stylized geometric work like this.

A key discovery was how naturally Geometry Nodes lends itself to animation. Since everything in the node graph is driven by numeric values, introducing motion is as simple as animating the W property of a 4D noise texture. This produces smooth, organic movement with no manual keyframing required.

The lighting was carefully considered to create strong contrast between highlights and shadows, using a glossy metal material paired with directional lighting and a bloom effect — the same optical phenomenon that makes streetlights appear soft at night. Getting the lighting right was one of the most satisfying parts of the process.

## Next steps

If I were to take this further, I'd love to create a longer motion graphic combining multiple Geometry Nodes setups to tell a visual story. Motion graphics blend procedural animation with narrative — the kind of thing you see in explainer videos and brand advertisements — and this technique would be a compelling medium to explore next.
